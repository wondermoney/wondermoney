Use repayments to record what you have paid toward a loan or credit facility.

This keeps the remaining balance aligned with recorded payments.

## What to record

- Repayment amount
- Date
- Notes, if needed

Record the repayment on the date it actually cleared when you want the balance history to stay accurate.

## What the view tells you

- How much of the balance remains.
- How repayment activity changes the liability over time.
- Whether a repayment has already been recorded.

## Common mistakes

- Recording the same repayment twice.
- Entering a repayment against the wrong loan.
- Assuming a repayment changes a goal balance. Loans and goals are separate.

If a payment was refunded or reversed, note that before adding a new repayment entry.

## Correct the balance

When your lender's statement shows a different balance, record their figure. On the loan page, choose **Adjust balance**, keep **Correct balance**, and enter the balance and the date of the statement. Zero is allowed, for a loan the lender reports as paid off.

- A correction replaces the tracked balance from its date on; it is not an extra payment. Repayments before that date are already in the lender's figure, and repayments after it reduce the corrected balance.
- A correction dated in the future does not change today's balance. It takes effect on its date.
- The other types change the balance by an amount: a fee or capitalized interest adds to it, and a principal decrease or a write-off reduces it.

Every adjustment is listed under **Balance adjustments**, where you can edit or delete it. The balance, the schedule and the payoff projection follow.

## Record rate changes

For a variable-rate loan, set the rate type to **Variable** when you add or edit the loan, and enter the rate the loan started with. Then, on the loan page, add each change under **Rate history** with the date it takes effect.

- Each month's interest uses the rate in force when that month began. When a new rate is in force on a payment date, the payment is recalculated so the loan still ends on its maturity date.
- WonderMoney does not fetch index rates such as Euribor or predict changes. Add each change when your lender announces it.
