Loans and credit facilities can be tracked in WonderMoney as obligations you want to follow over time.
Use this area to keep an eye on what you owe, what has been repaid, and which transactions may be linked to a repayment.

At a high level, a loan record helps you:

- keep the outstanding balance visible
- note the repayment schedule or expected cadence
- review repayments as they happen
- connect account transactions that may represent a repayment

Balances should be treated as the current tracking amount in WonderMoney, not a substitute for your lender’s official statement.
If the numbers differ, rely on the lender as the source of truth.

## Repayments and balances

Each repayment reduces the tracked balance over time.
You can use WonderMoney to record repayments manually or review imported transactions that look relevant.

When you update a repayment, the goal is to keep the remaining balance and payment history easy to review.
This is especially useful when a loan has variable payments, extra repayments, or irregular charges.

## Repayment candidates and links

WonderMoney may surface repayment candidates from your connected accounts.
These are transactions that appear to match a loan repayment based on their amount, date, or description.

Review candidates carefully before linking them.
Linking a transaction does not change the lender’s records; it only helps WonderMoney relate the transaction to the loan.

Use links when you want to:

- match a bank transaction to a specific repayment
- avoid counting the same payment twice
- keep the loan timeline easier to follow

## Amortization methods

A loan's schedule shows every instalment: how much is interest, how much reduces the principal, and the balance left after it.
Rows with a linked repayment are marked as paid, so you can see whether the loan is ahead or behind the contract.

WonderMoney supports two common methods:

- **French (level payment).** The instalment stays the same for the whole term. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.
- **Constant principal (SAC).** The same principal is repaid every month. Interest falls with the balance, so the instalment starts higher and falls over time.

Choose the method when you add or edit a loan. The schedule uses the original loan amount, interest rate and term; if one of them is missing, the page explains what to add.

## Payoff projection

The loan page projects when the balance reaches zero at the contract payment, and how much sooner an extra monthly payment gets you there.
Enter an extra amount to compare the two balance lines; the projection updates as you type.

The projection starts from the current outstanding principal, so linked repayments and balance adjustments are already reflected.
It uses the contract instalment from the schedule and adds the extra each month.
The summary shows the projected payoff date, the months saved and the interest saved against the contract-only plan.

If the payment does not cover the monthly interest, the page says the loan does not pay off instead of showing an impossible date.
This is an estimate from the figures stored in WonderMoney, not a settlement quote from your lender.

## Link a loan to the asset it finances

A mortgage finances a home and a car loan a car. When you add or edit a loan, choose the asset under **Secured against**: one of your household's [homes, vehicles and other assets](/en/docs/portfolio-investments/manual-assets). Choose None to remove the link.

- A loan is secured against one asset at most. One asset can have several loans, such as a first and a second mortgage.
- The asset's page then shows its value today, what is still owed on the loans secured against it, and the equity: the value less that debt. The loan's page names the asset.
- The debt is each loan's outstanding principal today, after its linked repayments and balance adjustments. A loan in another currency is converted into the asset's currency at the latest exchange rate, including a rate your household set itself. When no rate exists, the asset's page says which currency is missing and shows no equity, rather than adding amounts in different currencies.
- Loans are personal, so the equity you see counts only your own loans on the asset.
- The link does not change your net worth: the asset still counts at its value and the loan at its balance, each once.
- Deleting the asset removes the link and keeps the loan as it is.
- The mobile app has the same choice in the loan form and shows the equity on the asset's page.

## What this does not do

WonderMoney is not a lender and does not replace your loan agreement, repayment notice, or official statement.
It is a help tool for organising your own view of the debt.
