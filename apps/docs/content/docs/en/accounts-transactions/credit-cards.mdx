A credit card's spending belongs to a statement that closes on a fixed day and is paid on a due day. WonderMoney uses those two days to decide which statement each transaction belongs to, so you can see what you owe now, when it is due and how much credit is left.

## Before you start

Find the two days on your card's statement or in your banking app: the day the statement closes and the day the payment is due. Both are days of the month, from 1 to 31.

## Steps

1. Open the account and choose **Edit**.
2. Turn on **Credit card account**.
3. Enter the day the statement closes and the day the payment is due.
4. Enter the credit limit, if you want to see the credit still available. The minimum payment is optional.
5. Save.

The account's page then shows the current statement: its balance, its due date and the credit still available. A statement balance is what the statement charged minus the payments and refunds counted against it.

## Month ends and the close day

A close day past the end of a shorter month uses that month's last day. A card that closes on the 31st closes on 28 February in a common year and on 29 February in a leap year.

A transaction made on the close day belongs to the statement that closes that day. The day after the close starts the next statement.

The due date is the first due day after the close. Closing on the 5th and paying on the 25th is due the same month; closing on the 20th and paying on the 10th is due the next month.

## Moving a transaction to another statement

When your bank's statement disagrees with its own transaction list, you can move a single transaction. Open the transaction and set its statement date: it is counted in the statement that contains that date instead of the one its own date falls in. Clearing the date puts it back.

## Synced accounts

When your bank reports an account as a card, a synced account is marked as a credit card automatically. Set the close day, due day and limit yourself, because banks rarely share the cycle.

## Common issues

- The statement total looks lower than the card balance: a statement counts only its own period, while the balance carries everything.
- A payment made after the close appears in the next statement.
- A refund counted as a payment reduces the statement balance.
- A card with no limit set shows no available credit.

## Statement history and reminders

On the web, a card's account page has a **Statements** tab; on mobile the statements are listed under the current statement on the account screen. Select a statement to see the transactions assigned to it. Each statement is marked **Open** until it closes, **Closed** once it has closed with a balance still to pay, **Paid** when payments cover what it charged, or **Overdue** when it is still unpaid after its due date. A payment goes to the oldest statement that still has a balance, and any amount beyond that balance carries forward to the next one.

A closed statement that still has a balance gets one email reminder a few days before it is due: three days by default. If a day is missed, the reminder goes out on the next day, up to the due date itself. A statement that is paid before then gets no reminder. The reminder goes to the person who owns the card, in their account language: English, Portuguese or Spanish.

Reminders are on by default. Turn off **Card payment reminders** in **Settings → Notifications** to stop them; unsubscribing from all emails stops them too. Reminders are sent by email only.

On a self-hosted instance, reminders need an email provider (see [Configure your instance](/en/docs/self-hosting/configure#email)). Set `CREDIT_CARD_DUE_REMINDER_DAYS` on the worker to change how many days before the due date they are sent.

## Installment purchases

In Brazil and Portugal a card purchase is often paid in fixed monthly installments: the bank shows `PARC 03/12` or `3/12` on the third of twelve. An installment plan records the purchase once and lays out its installments, one per statement, so you can see what the card still has to charge.

### Create a plan

1. Open the card's account page. The **Installments** section is below the statement summary.
2. Choose **New installment plan** (on mobile, **Add plan**).
3. Enter a description, the purchase date, the total and the number of installments (2 to 120). A category is optional.
4. Save.

The installments are the total divided by their number, rounded down to the cent; the last one takes what is left, so they always add up to the total exactly. 100 in 3 installments is 33.33, 33.33 and 33.34. The first installment is in the statement that contains the purchase date and each next one in the following statement, dated by the card's close and due days. On an account with no statement cycle they fall monthly on the purchase's day of the month.

### Future installments are projections

Installments still to come are projections, like the balance forecast. They are not transactions: they do not change the account balance, net worth, the transaction lists or reports. They appear as upcoming items in the plan, in the card's **Still to be charged** amount, in the balance forecast and on the bills calendar.

When the bank posts an installment, WonderMoney links the posted transaction to its installment, which then reads as **Paid**. Nothing is counted twice, because the projection was never a transaction. A posted row is linked when it is on the plan's card, its description names the installment (`PARC 03/12` on a 12-installment plan), and its amount matches. Transactions in the list show the installment as `3/12`.

A past installment that no posted transaction was linked to shows as **Not matched**. It is no longer counted in what the card still has to charge. You can link its transaction by hand.

### Link a transaction by hand

WonderMoney links a posted transaction by itself only when its marker, amount, card and date fit exactly one plan. When the bank writes no marker, rounds the amount differently or dates the installment elsewhere, link it yourself:

1. Open the plan. Next to a **Not matched** or **Upcoming** installment, choose **Link transaction** (on mobile, the installment's **…** menu).
2. The list shows posted charges on the plan's card within a month of the installment's date, for about its amount, closest first. A charge that differs says by how much. Choose **Show other amounts** to see every charge in that period.
3. Pick the transaction and choose **Link**. The installment reads as **Paid**, and the card's **Still to be charged** amount, the forecast and the transaction lists update.

You can also start from the transaction: in a card's transaction list, open a posted charge and choose **Link to installment** to pick one of the open installments of that card's plans.

A transaction pays one installment only, on its own card. WonderMoney says so when you pick a transaction from another card, one that already pays an installment, or an installment that another transaction has paid in the meantime.

To undo a link, choose **Unlink** next to a **Paid** installment and confirm. The transaction stays on the card as an ordinary transaction, and the installment shows as **Not matched** or **Upcoming** again. An unlinked charge stays unlinked: later syncs and imports don't link it to that plan again, though you can still link it by hand.

### Budgets

A posted installment counts in the budget month of its own statement: the month its installment is scheduled in. This holds even when the bank dates every installment on the original purchase day, so a purchase in 12 installments never lands in one month's budget. Installments still to come count in no budget until the bank posts them.

### Suggested plans

When posted transactions on a card carry installment markers such as `PARC 01/04` and `PARC 02/04`, the card's page lists them under **Possible installment purchases** (on mobile, **Detected installments**). Choose **Create plan** to open the form filled in from those transactions, check it, and save. The plan is created only when you save it, and the transactions it came from are linked to their installments.

### Change or delete a plan

Open a plan to see each installment with its date, statement due date, amount and status. You can change its description or category, or delete it. Deleting a plan keeps its posted transactions; they are only no longer linked to it.
