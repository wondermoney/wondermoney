title: Credit cards
description: Set a card's statement cycle and see what you owe this month.

A credit card's spending belongs to a statement that closes on a fixed day and is paid on a due day. WonderMoney uses those two days to decide which statement each transaction belongs to, so you can see what you owe now, when it is due and how much credit is left.

## Before you start

Find the two days on your card's statement or in your banking app: the day the statement closes and the day the payment is due. Both are days of the month, from 1 to 31.

## Steps

1. Open the account and choose **Edit**.
2. Turn on **Credit card account**.
3. Enter the day the statement closes and the day the payment is due.
4. Enter the credit limit, if you want to see the credit still available. The minimum payment is optional.
5. Save.

The account's page then shows the current statement: its balance, its due date and the credit still available. A statement balance is what the statement charged minus the payments and refunds counted against it.

## Month ends and the close day

A close day past the end of a shorter month uses that month's last day. A card that closes on the 31st closes on 28 February in a common year and on 29 February in a leap year.

A transaction made on the close day belongs to the statement that closes that day. The day after the close starts the next statement.

The due date is the first due day after the close. Closing on the 5th and paying on the 25th is due the same month; closing on the 20th and paying on the 10th is due the next month.

## Moving a transaction to another statement

When your bank's statement disagrees with its own transaction list, you can move a single transaction. Open the transaction and set its statement date: it is counted in the statement that contains that date instead of the one its own date falls in. Clearing the date puts it back.

## Synced accounts

When your bank reports an account as a card, a synced account is marked as a credit card automatically. Set the close day, due day and limit yourself, because banks rarely share the cycle.

## Common issues

- The statement total looks lower than the card balance: a statement counts only its own period, while the balance carries everything.
- A payment made after the close appears in the next statement.
- A refund counted as a payment reduces the statement balance.
- A card with no limit set shows no available credit.

## Statement history and reminders

On the web, a card's account page has a **Statements** tab; on mobile the statements are listed under the current statement on the account screen. Select a statement to see the transactions assigned to it. Each statement is marked **Open** until it closes, **Closed** once it has closed with a balance still to pay, **Paid** when payments cover what it charged, or **Overdue** when it is still unpaid after its due date. A payment goes to the oldest statement that still has a balance, and any amount beyond that balance carries forward to the next one.

A closed statement that still has a balance gets one email reminder a few days before it is due: three days by default. If a day is missed, the reminder goes out on the next day, up to the due date itself. A statement that is paid before then gets no reminder. The reminder goes to the person who owns the card, in English or Portuguese.

Reminders are on by default. Turn off **Card payment reminders** in **Settings → Notifications** to stop them; unsubscribing from all emails stops them too. Reminders are sent by email only.

On a self-hosted instance, reminders need an email provider (see [Configure your instance](/en/docs/self-hosting/configure#email)). Set `CREDIT_CARD_DUE_REMINDER_DAYS` on the worker to change how many days before the due date they are sent.
