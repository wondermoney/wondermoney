title: Recurring payments
description: Recognize and manage repeating income and expenses.

Recurring payments are transactions that happen on a regular schedule, such as rent, subscriptions, salary, or loan repayments. Marking them correctly helps you understand what repeats and what changes from month to month.

## Before you start

Review several months of activity if possible. A recurring item is easier to confirm when you compare the same date pattern across more than one statement.

## Steps

1. Open a transaction that repeats.
2. Confirm the merchant, amount pattern, and timing.
3. Mark it as recurring or group it with the matching series.
4. Review the next occurrence to make sure it still fits.

## Review

Check recurring items before budget planning so fixed costs and expected income are clear. Update the series when the amount changes or the payment stops.

## Calendar

The calendar shows every confirmed recurring payment on the day it falls this month. Use the arrows to move between months, and select a day to see the payments on it.

Each payment is marked paid, due, overdue or projected:

- **Paid**: a matching transaction was recorded.
- **Due**: expected today and not paid yet.
- **Overdue**: expected on an earlier day and not paid.
- **Projected**: expected later this month.

A day's total appears only when all of its payments are in the same currency. Payments on a private account appear only to the people who can see that account.

## Forecast

An account's page projects its balance for the next 30 days: today's balance plus the confirmed recurring payments still to come. The balance chart continues after today as a dashed line, and the low point callout names the lowest projected balance and the day it falls on.

Projected payments also appear at the top of the account's transaction list, marked as projections. They are not transactions: they are not counted in reports, budgets or totals, and nothing is recorded until you add the payment yourself. A bill already paid — even a few days early — drops out of the forecast, so it never counts twice.

Each account is projected in its own currency. When the household has accounts in more than one currency, the total is shown per currency rather than as one mixed figure. A private account's forecast is visible only to the people who can see the account.

## Common issues

- A subscription amount changes slightly each month.
- A seasonal charge looks recurring but only happens once a quarter.
- A salary payment lands a day earlier or later than expected.
- The same series was tagged in more than one account.
