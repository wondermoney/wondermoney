---
title: Homes, Vehicles and Other Assets
description: Count a home, a car or a valuable in your net worth at a value you set, and keep a history of what it was worth.
---

Some of what you own has no bank feed and no market price: a home, a car, a piece of jewellery. Add it as an asset, say what it is worth, and update the value when that changes. It counts in your net worth from the day of its first valuation.

## Add an asset

1. Open Assets. It sits under Investments in the sidebar; on a phone, under More.
2. Choose Add asset.
3. Enter a name and choose the kind: Property, Vehicle, Valuable or Other.
4. Enter the current value, its currency, and the day it was valued.
5. If you like, add the purchase price and date, a yearly change, notes, and whether the asset is private.
6. Save.

With a purchase price and a purchase date earlier than the valuation, the purchase is also recorded as a valuation. The history then starts at the purchase, and your net worth does not jump by the whole value on the day you add the asset.

The currency cannot be changed once the asset exists, because every valuation is in it.

For Bitcoin or Ethereum, add a crypto wallet instead: its value is read from its public addresses every day. See [Crypto wallets](/en/docs/portfolio-investments/crypto-wallets).

## Import several assets from a file

On the Assets page, choose **Import CSV** and download the template: one row per asset with its name, kind, value, valuation date, currency and whether it is private. Every asset is created with that value as its first valuation, as if you had added it by hand, or none is when a row has a problem. See [Import Portfolio CSV](/en/docs/portfolio-investments/import-portfolio-csv#import-manual-assets).

## Update the value

Open the asset and choose Update value. Enter what it is worth and on which day.

Recording a value never creates a transaction. It does not touch your cash flow, budgets, or income and spending reports: only the asset's value and your net worth change.

- An asset has one valuation per day. A value for a day that already has one replaces it.
- Edit or remove a valuation from the Valuations list on the asset's page. An asset always keeps at least one valuation; to stop counting it, archive or delete it.

## In the mobile app

Assets is under More in the mobile app. There you can see your assets and their value history, add an asset and update its value. Editing, archiving and deleting an asset, and correcting or removing a valuation, are on the web.

## How the value is worked out

- The value on a day is the latest valuation on or before that day. Between valuations it stays the same, so the chart steps on the day of each valuation.
- With a yearly change, the value moves by that percentage a year from the latest valuation (or from the Changes from date, when that is later), in proportion to the days that have passed. At 5% a year, 100,000 becomes 105,000 after one year and 110,250 after two. Use a negative percentage for something that loses value, such as a car.
- A value worked out from the yearly change is marked as estimated on the asset's page. The next valuation you record replaces the estimate, and the yearly change carries on from it.

## Assets in your net worth

- Assets count on the assets side of your net worth, on the home screen, in your net worth history and in the net worth history report.
- An asset in another currency is converted into yours with the European Central Bank's daily rates. Each day of the history uses the rate in force on that day. When no rate exists for the asset's currency, the asset keeps its own value but is left out of the totals, and the totals say so. It is never added as if the amount were already in your currency.
- **Archive** an asset you sold or no longer have. It stops counting from the day you archive it, and your earlier net worth history keeps it. Bring it back to count it again.
- **Delete** removes the asset and all its valuations, including from your net worth history. It cannot be undone.

## Private assets in a household

An asset is shared with your household unless you make it private. Everyone in the household can see a shared asset, and everyone but a viewer can revalue, edit, archive and delete it.

A private asset is visible only to the member who owns it. The other members do not see it on the Assets page, and it is not in their net worth today or in their history. Only the member who owns it can make an asset private or shared.

## If you used a manual account for this

Before assets existed, a manual account of type Other was the way to count a home, but its balance only moves through transactions, so a new value showed up as income or spending. If you add the same home as an asset, close that account: otherwise your net worth counts the home twice. WonderMoney does not detect this for you.

## Loans

A mortgage or a car loan stays a loan. Net worth counts the home or car on the assets side and the loan on the liabilities side.

To see your equity in a home, link the mortgage to it: in the loan's form, choose the home under **Secured against**. The asset's page then shows its value, what is still owed on the loans secured against it and the equity between them, in the asset's currency. The link changes nothing in your net worth. See [Loans overview](/en/docs/goals-loans/loans-overview).

## Export and restore

The full data export includes your assets and their valuations: the shared ones and your own private ones, and which of your loans each one secures. Restoring an export brings them back. See [Data and privacy](/en/docs/settings-security-privacy/data-privacy).
